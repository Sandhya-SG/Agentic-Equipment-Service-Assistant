## General Safety Principles
All service on the VP-200 must be performed in Service Mode with the stage servo
disabled. Never place hands near the stage while it is energized. Never bypass the
optics enclosure interlock. Tasks requiring access to energized electrical
sections must be escalated to a qualified engineer.

## Laser and Optical Radiation Hazards
The VP-200 alignment subsystem uses a Class 2 laser for target positioning. Never
look directly into the laser aperture or its reflection. Do not remove the laser
housing cover; alignment-laser service requires a qualified engineer and is not a
field task. Confirm the laser is disabled in Service Mode before working near the
optics path.

## High Voltage and Stored Energy
The lighting driver and the power supply unit operate at high voltage. Service of
these subsystems requires lockout/tagout and is restricted to qualified engineers.
Field service engineers must not open the power supply enclosure. The lighting
driver may retain stored energy in its capacitors after power off; a mandatory
discharge wait of five minutes applies, and this work must be escalated rather
than performed in the field.

## Pinch and Motion Hazards
The motorized stage presents a pinch hazard when energized. Always confirm the
stage servo is disabled before reaching into the stage area. Keep the optics
enclosure closed during any Calibration Mode routine that moves the stage.

## Required PPE Summary
Routine VP-200 service requires safety glasses at minimum, and powder-free gloves
when handling optics. Laser-path work, high voltage, stored energy, and power
supply access are not field-serviceable and must be escalated to a qualified
engineer.
