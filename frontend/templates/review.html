Reviewer help

1. Why a case is here

  • stopped: the assistant halted the request. A rule or the Safety Agent decided the action must not proceed.
  • escalated: the evidence was not enough, or the task is hazardous, so a person must take over.
  • escalate-only hazard: the question names high voltage or stored energy. No procedure is given, only a referral.
  • low confidence: the answer was given, but the assistant was not confident enough in it.
  • quality sample: an ordinary answer picked at random for a quality check.

2. Understanding the assistant's decision

  • halt: the requested action must not proceed. escalate: hand the task to a qualified person.
  • Confidence: how well the answer is supported by the manual passages. It is shown when the assistant gave an answer; a stop from a rule has none.
  • Hazards: what the safety rules found in the request or the answer. Each identifier is explained below.

3. Reviewing the evidence

  • Manual passages are the quotes the answer relied on, with document, page and section. Check that they say what the answer says.
  • None recorded is normal for a request a safety rule stopped: the rule acted before the manual was searched. It does not mean a citation was checked. The rule that applied is the hazard listed in the case.
  • The question is shown as the assistant received it, so you see the same words it did.

4. Making a decision

  • Confirm: the assistant's decision was right. Confirming a stop does not authorise the engineer to proceed; it records that the stop was right.
  • Correct: you write the corrected guidance. It must cite the manual (one source per line) and a reason, and it is checked like any answer. Guidance that tells the engineer to bypass, defeat or disable a safety control is refused. Never use Correct to get around a mandatory safety prohibition.
  • Reject: the answer is wrong, unsupported or unsuitable. A reason of at least 5 characters is required. The engineer is told the answer was withdrawn and must not rely on it.

5. Safety and accountability

  • Every decision is recorded with your name, the time and your reason in the audit trail. A case takes one decision only.
  • Nothing is executed on the equipment. The engineer reads your outcome on the assistant page and decides what to do.
  • Times on this page are in UTC (Singapore time is eight hours ahead).
